# Exact Residue Cascade After the u-Method Correction

**Date:** 2026-08-28

This note replaces the old three-target cascade with the exact divisor criterion and records the additional forced-factor audit performed on the deterministic residue argument.

## 1. Correct reduced criterion

Let \(u\equiv3\pmod4\), \(x=(n+u)/4\), and \(D=(nx)^2\).  The u-method requires a divisor \(d\mid D\) such that

\[
d\equiv -nx\pmod u.
\]

If \(\gcd(nx,u)=1\), the complementary divisor automatically has the same residue, since

\[
D=(nx)^2\equiv(-nx)^2\pmod u.
\]

For prime \(n\) and prime \(u\nmid n\), write \(x=\prod p_i^{e_i}\).  The exact centered criterion is

\[
n^a\prod p_i^{b_i}\equiv-1\pmod u,
\]

with

\[
a\in\{-1,0,1\},\qquad -e_i\le b_i\le e_i.
\]

Equivalently, reducing to divisors \(e\mid x^2\), the exact targets are

\[
\boxed{-x,\;-xn,\;-xn^{-1}}\pmod u,
\]

not \(\{-x,-xn,-xn^2\}\).  The old third target agrees with the correct one only when \(n^3\equiv1\pmod u\).

## 2. Full diagnosis through 10^7

For all 48,711 prime hard cases through \(10^7\):

- the exact verifier has **0 failures** with \(u\le300\);
- **405** first-working values differ from the old shortcut;
- all 405 old false first hits occur at the old **j=2** target;
- all 405 satisfy \(n^3\not\equiv1\pmod{u_{\rm old}}\);
- every changed case lies in \(n\equiv1\) or \(49\pmod{120}\).

Thus the old j=0 and j=1 reductions were valid; the old j=2 formula was the sole source of the discrepancy.

## 3. Forced-factor audit

A second correction is necessary when using a known factor of \(x\).  If a prime \(p\mid x\), one must write

\[
x=pq.
\]

The safe guaranteed divisors of \(x^2\) are then generated by

\[
p^c q^b,\qquad b,c\in\{0,1,2\}.
\]

It is generally invalid to treat both \(x\) and \(p\) as independent generators and use \(x^b p^c\), because this can require a larger exponent of \(p\) than is present in \(x^2\).  For several forced factors, write \(x=Pq\) with \(P\) the product of the distinct guaranteed factors and use \(q\) and those factors as the independent divisor basis.

All deterministic residue results below use this audited basis.

## 4. Exact deterministic coverage modulo 120

The prime hard-case candidate classes modulo 120 are

\[
1,13,37,49,61,73,97,109.
\]

Using only factors among \(2,3,5\) whose divisibility of \((n+u)/4\) is forced by the class, the exact audited criterion gives a universal fixed-u route for six of the eight classes.  The only base classes with no universal hit are

\[
\boxed{n\equiv1,49\pmod{120}}.
\]

This remains unchanged after the forced-factor audit and exactly matches the classes in which the old and corrected first-working values differ.

Examples of universal audited routes include:

- \(n\equiv13\pmod{120}\): \(u=7\) using the forced factor 5;
- \(n\equiv73\pmod{120}\): \(u=7\) using forced factors 2 and 5;
- \(n\equiv109\pmod{120}\): \(u=11\) using forced factors 2, 3, and 5.

## 5. Rigorous u=7 QNR reduction

For the two residual classes, \(n\equiv1\pmod8\).  Hence

\[
x_7=\frac{n+7}{4}
\]

is even.  Write \(x_7=2q\).  Genuine guaranteed divisors have the form

\[
n^a q^b2^c,\qquad a,b,c\in\{0,1,2\}.
\]

Finite arithmetic in \((\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z)^\times\) gives the exact failure residues

\[
\{1,2,4\}=\mathrm{QR}_7.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\left(\frac n7\right)=-1\Longrightarrow u=7\text{ succeeds}.}
\]

Any unresolved prime in the two residual modulo-120 classes must consequently satisfy

\[
\boxed{\left(\frac n7\right)=+1.}
\]

The analogous \(u=11\) and \(u=23\) forced-factor routes eliminate additional explicit QNR residue classes, but they do not cover all quadratic nonresidues; see `QNR_REDUCTION.md`.

## 6. Refinement modulo 120120

Refine the residual classes modulo

\[
M=\operatorname{lcm}(120,28,44,52)=120120,
\]

which records the residue information needed for forced factors \(2,3,5,7,11,13\).

Among residue classes coprime to \(2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13\) and lying above \(1\) or \(49\pmod{120}\):

- admissible classes: **1,440**;
- deterministically covered by the audited guaranteed-factor cascade with prime \(u\equiv3\pmod4\), \(u\le127\): **1,145**;
- remaining: **295**.

Every one of the 295 remaining classes satisfies

\[
\left(\frac n7\right)=+1.
\]

Their further distribution is:

- modulo 11: **250 QR**, **45 QNR**;
- modulo 13: **210 QR**, **85 QNR**.

Thus \(\mathrm{QR}_7\) is the first exact subgroup barrier.  The later moduli stratify that barrier but do not by themselves give complete QNR implications.

## 7. Current theorem target

The remaining problem is now more precise:

\[
\boxed{
 n\equiv1\text{ or }49\pmod{120},\qquad
 \left(\frac n7\right)=1,
}
\]

together with a finite list of additional residue restrictions coming from the audited \(u=11,23,\ldots\) routes.

The most promising deterministic direction is to prove that one of the forced factors of \((n+u)/4\), after being separated from the cofactor exactly, expands the centered product set enough to hit \(-1\) in \((\mathbb Z/u\mathbb Z)^\times\).
