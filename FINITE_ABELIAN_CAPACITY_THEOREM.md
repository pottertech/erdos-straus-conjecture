# Finite-Abelian Capacity Form of the Exact Centered Criterion

**Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Status:** Exact reformulation for arbitrary u with `gcd(nx,u)=1`

## 1. Setup

Let

\[
x=\frac{n+u}{4},\qquad M=nx,
\]

and assume

\[
\gcd(M,u)=1.
\]

The exact centered theorem says that the u-method succeeds if and only if

\[
\prod_{p^e\parallel M}p^{c_p}\equiv-1\pmod u,
\qquad -e\le c_p\le e.
\]

All residues therefore lie in the finite abelian unit group

\[
G_u=(\mathbb Z/u\mathbb Z)^\times.
\]

## 2. Coordinate decomposition

Choose any invariant-factor decomposition

\[
G_u\cong C_{m_1}\times\cdots\times C_{m_r},
\qquad m_1\mid m_2\mid\cdots\mid m_r.
\]

Let

\[
\phi:G_u\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z/m_1\mathbb Z\times\cdots\times\mathbb Z/m_r\mathbb Z
\]

be a fixed isomorphism.

For each prime `p|M`, write its coordinate vector as

\[
v_p=\phi(p\bmod u),
\]

and write

\[
t=\phi(-1\bmod u).
\]

## Theorem (finite-abelian capacity criterion)

The u-method succeeds if and only if there are integers `c_p` satisfying

\[
-v_p(M)\le c_p\le v_p(M)
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\mid M} c_p v_p=t
\quad\text{in}\quad
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb Z/m_i\mathbb Z.
}
\]

### Proof

Under the group isomorphism `phi`, multiplication in `G_u` becomes coordinatewise addition.  Therefore

\[
\phi\left(\prod p^{c_p}\right)
=
\sum c_p\phi(p)
=
\sum c_p v_p.
\]

The product equals `-1 mod u` exactly when its coordinate vector equals `t`.  The exponent bounds are unchanged from the exact centered theorem. ∎

## 3. Capacity compression by inverse classes

If several prime residues have coordinate vectors equal to `v` or `-v`, their exponent intervals can be aggregated.

Suppose the total valuation in the inverse pair `{v,-v}` is `A_v`.  Then all aggregate coefficients

\[
a_v\in[-A_v,A_v]\cap\mathbb Z
\]

are attainable in direction `v`.  Thus the prime-by-prime box can be compressed to one bounded integer variable per inverse pair of unit-group elements.

Self-inverse elements (`2v=0`) are handled similarly, noting that only the coefficient modulo their order matters.

The exact problem becomes a finite bounded linear system in the invariant-factor coordinates.

## 4. Specializations already used in the project

### Prime u

For prime `u`, `G_u` is cyclic of order `u-1`; the theorem reduces to `PRIME_MODULUS_CAPACITY_THEOREM.md`.

### Odd prime powers

For `u=p^k` with odd prime p, `G_u` is cyclic.  Therefore gates such as `u=27` admit the same one-dimensional discrete-log capacity treatment, with group order

\[
\varphi(27)=18.
\]

### u=15

\[
G_{15}\cong C_4\times C_2.
\]

Choosing basis elements 2 and 11 gives exactly the coordinate analysis in `MOD15_CAPACITY_CLASSIFICATION.md`.

### General squarefree composite gates

For gates such as 35, 39, 51, 55, 87, and 95, CRT gives

\[
G_u\cong\prod_{p\mid u}G_p.
\]

The capacity theorem can therefore be implemented directly by a vector of discrete logs in each prime component, without inventing a nonexistent single primitive root modulo u.

## 5. Subgroup-purity obstruction

Let `H` be a subgroup of `G_u` containing every prime-factor residue of `M`.  Then every centered product lies in H.  Consequently, if

\[
-1\notin H,
\]

then u must fail.

This single statement unifies several observed obstructions:

- prime-u factorwise QR purity: `H` is the quadratic-residue subgroup;
- u=15 residual purity: `H=<2>={1,2,4,8}`;
- other composite gates can be classified by proper subgroups avoiding `-1`.

The converse need not hold: even when `-1` belongs to the subgroup generated by the factor residues, bounded exponent capacities may still be too small.  Those are the finite-capacity exceptional vectors seen at u=11 and u=19.

## 6. Algorithmic consequence

For fixed u, an exact verifier can avoid enumerating divisors of `M^2`:

1. factor M;
2. map each prime factor to its invariant-factor coordinate vector;
3. aggregate valuations by inverse coordinate class;
4. solve the bounded finite-group subset-sum problem for target `phi(-1)`.

Because u is small in the cascade, the state space is bounded by `|G_u|=phi(u)` and dynamic programming is trivial.  This gives a uniform exact implementation for every current gate.

## 7. Proof strategy suggested by the data

A hypothetical counterexample must make every shifted product

\[
M_u=n(n+u)/4
\]

land in one of the finite failure states for every gate u.

Those states split naturally into:

1. **subgroup-pure states**, where all factor residues lie in a proper subgroup omitting -1;
2. **capacity exceptions**, where the generated subgroup contains -1 but the available prime valuations are too small to reach it.

The corrected 10^7 data show that subgroup-pure states dominate.  The proof problem is therefore no longer an amorphous divisor-distribution question: it is to rule out simultaneous persistence of an explicit sequence of subgroup/capacity conditions on the shifted products `M_u`.
