# Deterministic QNR Reduction for the Corrected u-Method

**Date:** 2026-08-28

For the two residual prime hard-case classes

\[
n\equiv1,49\pmod{120},
\]

we have \(n\equiv1\pmod8\).  The exact u-method criterion asks for a divisor
\(d\mid (nx)^2\) with \(d\equiv-nx\pmod u\), where \(x=(n+u)/4\).

A forced factor must be separated correctly.  If \(p\mid x\), write \(x=pq\); then the guaranteed divisor basis of \(x^2\) is generated by \(p\) and \(q\), each with exponent in \(\{0,1,2\}\).  Treating both \(x\) and \(p\) as independent generators can overrun the exponent of \(p\) available in \(x^2\), so that shortcut is invalid.

## Exact u = 7 lemma

Since \(n\equiv1\pmod8\),

\[
x_7=\frac{n+7}{4}
\]

is even.  Write \(x_7=2q\).  The guaranteed divisors

\[
n^a q^b2^c,\qquad a,b,c\in\{0,1,2\},
\]

are genuine divisors of \((nx_7)^2\).  Direct finite arithmetic in \((\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z)^\times\) shows that the target \(-nx_7\) fails precisely for

\[
\{1,2,4\}=\mathrm{QR}_7.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\left(\frac n7\right)=-1\Longrightarrow u=7\text{ succeeds}.}
\]

Consequently, any prime in the two residual classes that is not solved by this route must satisfy

\[
\boxed{\left(\frac n7\right)=+1.}
\]

## u = 11 and u = 23 are partial QNR routes

For \(u=11\), \(3\mid(n+11)/4\).  After separating that factor correctly, the forced-factor failure residues are

\[
\{1,2,3,4,5,6,9\},
\]

so the guaranteed route succeeds for \(n\equiv7,8,10\pmod{11}\).  These successful residues are quadratic nonresidues, but not every QNR modulo 11 is covered.

For \(u=23\), both 2 and 3 divide \((n+23)/4\).  With the corrected basis, the failure residues are

\[
\{1,2,3,4,5,6,8,9,12,13,14,16,18\},
\]

and the guaranteed route succeeds for

\[
\{7,10,11,15,17,19,20,21,22\}\pmod{23}.
\]

Again every listed success is a QNR, but the route does not cover all QNR residues.

Thus the rigorous global subgroup reduction presently established is the \(u=7\) lemma.  The mod-11 and mod-23 routes provide additional deterministic residue exclusions after refinement, but they must not be summarized as full Legendre-symbol implications.
