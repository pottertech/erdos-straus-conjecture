# Two-Branch Dihedral Residue Graph

**Status:** exact local combinatorial analysis

At a fixed shift `u`, the untwisted and twisted unit-partner targets are

\[
t_1=-1,
\qquad
t_2=-n^{-1}
\pmod u.
\]

Define the two involutions on the unit group

\[
\tau_1(r)=t_1r^{-1}=-r^{-1},
\qquad
\tau_2(r)=t_2r^{-1}=-n^{-1}r^{-1}.
\]

If prime-factor residue classes of `K=(n+u)/4` contain both endpoints of an edge of `tau_1`, their product gives an untwisted divisor certificate. If they contain both endpoints of an edge of `tau_2`, their product gives a twisted divisor certificate.

Thus simultaneous failure forces the occupied residue set to be independent in the graph formed by the union of these two involution matchings (with fixed points treated separately when present).

## Dihedral composition

A direct calculation gives

\[
\tau_1\circ\tau_2(r)=nr,
\qquad
\tau_2\circ\tau_1(r)=n^{-1}r.
\]

Therefore the group generated by `tau_1,tau_2` is the dihedral action generated by multiplication by `n` and either reflection.

When both involutions are fixed-point-free, each connected component of their union is an even cycle (or, in the degenerate case where the two matchings coincide, a single matching edge).

Hence the maximum independent-set density of a component is

\[
\frac12.
\]

Consequently, even when both the untwisted and twisted involutions are fixed-point-free, simultaneous failure implies at most a half-sized occupied residue set, but **does not force a quarter-sized set**.

## Prime-shift interpretation

For prime `u == 3 (mod 4)`:

- `tau_1` is always fixed-point-free;
- `tau_2` is fixed-point-free exactly when `(n/u)=+1`.

In the quadratic-residue case, the clean two-branch graph is therefore a union of even cycles. The twisted branch changes the adjacency structure and the actual blind sets, but it does not automatically add another `1/2` to the sieve dimension at that same shift.

When `(n/u)=-1`, `tau_2` has two fixed points because `-n^{-1}` is a square. Then occupancy alone is insufficient: multiplicities at the fixed-point residue classes matter, since two available factors in the same fixed-point class would already multiply to the twisted target.

## Consequence for the analytic strategy

A correct multi-shift sieve may add approximately one half-dimension **per independent shift**, as in the untwisted argument and Dahan's fixed-depth theorem. It should not count the two unit-partner branches at one shift as two independent half-dimensional sieves.

The benefit of the twisted branch is instead:

1. it solves cases lying in the untwisted blind set;
2. it changes the local independent-set geometry;
3. it may reduce constants or eliminate specific residue profiles;
4. it is essential for the striking finite `u <= 299` coverage.

Any claim of dimension doubling at a single shift is therefore withdrawn.
