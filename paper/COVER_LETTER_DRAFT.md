# Journal Cover Letter Draft

Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript **“Prime-Cofactor Hierarchies in the Exact Type-II Erdős--Straus Depth Spectrum”** for publication.

The paper studies exact first occurrence in a classical divisor-square coordinate for prime Type-II solutions of the Erdős--Straus equation. The coordinate and the underlying Type-II parametrizations are prior mathematics; the manuscript does not claim a new parametrization and does not claim a proof of the Erdős--Straus conjecture.

The principal result is a fixed-cofactor finite-exception theorem. For every fixed prime cofactor `s>3`, the paper gives explicit congruence data for a variable prime `r` such that, apart from finitely many exceptional parameters, the depth

\[
A=(sr+1)/4
\]

is realized as the exact first-hit square-completion depth by infinitely many primes. The proof reduces all possible earlier collisions to layers containing the variable prime and derives the Diophantine condition

\[
r(kh-dc^2)+k+dh-2dc=0.
\]

A separate divisor-reuse lemma handles the degenerate `k=0` case; the remaining positive solutions are bounded by an AM--GM argument, leaving only finitely many exceptional parameter primes for each fixed cofactor.

The hierarchy gives a convergent lower-bound contribution to the spectrum of depth values realized infinitely often. A finite certificate using fixed prime cofactors through `10^6` gives

\[
\liminf_{X\to\infty}
\frac{N_\infty(X)\log X}{X}
\ge 3.0353080580213976\ldots .
\]

The manuscript explicitly separates its contribution from the classical Thépault/Mordell/Mizony divisor-square mechanism and from modern complete Type-II criteria of Elsholtz--Tao and Pomerance--Weingartner. It also discusses closely related contemporary exact-depth, Jacobi-signature, and search-depth work. A targeted literature search has not located the exact fixed-cofactor finite-exception theorem stated here, but the manuscript makes no historical-priority claim beyond the mathematical statements it proves.

A reproducibility repository contains the internal proof audit, finite coefficient certificate, and independent exact-depth computations; the main theorem itself is symbolic and does not depend on those computations.

This manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.

Sincerely,

Skip Potter
