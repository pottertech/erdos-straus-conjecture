# Journal Strategy for v9

**Date:** 2026-08-31

This is a fit/positioning note, not a submission decision.

## Current paper profile

- 6-page research note / short article.
- Number theory / Diophantine equations / Egyptian fractions.
- Main result is structural and exact-depth oriented, not a proof of the Erdős--Straus conjecture.
- Uses elementary algebra, quadratic reciprocity, CRT/Dirichlet, and a finite-exception argument; the earlier `3q` context also invokes sieve theory.
- The strongest candidate contribution is the fixed-prime-cofactor exact-first-hit hierarchy.

## 1. Acta Arithmetica — strong fit if external review confirms novelty

Official scope: arithmetic / number theory. The journal accepts preprints and asks for PDF at submission, with TeX source requested after acceptance.

Why it fits:

- concise number-theory papers are normal;
- the manuscript is primarily arithmetic rather than computational;
- the theorem is an exact Diophantine/congruence structure result.

Risk:

- standards are high, and novelty relative to the active Erdős--Straus literature will matter greatly.

**Current ranking:** first-choice journal to consider after expert review.

## 2. The Ramanujan Journal — natural topical fit

Pomerance--Weingartner's 2026 Erdős--Straus--Schinzel paper appears in this journal, so editors/referees are likely to understand the subject and its current literature.

Why it fits:

- broad number theory scope;
- active publication of Diophantine and arithmetic work;
- direct topical precedent.

Risk:

- because closely related modern work already appeared there, the distinction from prior Type-II parametrizations must be especially clear.

**Current ranking:** strong alternative / potentially first choice depending on external feedback.

## 3. Journal of Number Theory — suitable but likely ambitious for the current short form

JNT covers the full spectrum of contemporary number theory and explicitly welcomes computational number theory. Its public description emphasizes high-quality articles with technical details included.

Why it fits:

- exact-depth and analytic number-theory context is squarely within scope;
- computational supplement can be accommodated conceptually.

Risk:

- the current six-page format may be too short/light unless the theorem is judged especially strong;
- a self-contained expansion of the Type-II coordinate and no-reuse proof may improve fit.

**Current ranking:** ambitious option after expert validation and manuscript expansion.

## 4. INTEGERS — safer fallback

INTEGERS publishes original research in number theory, including elementary, multiplicative, probabilistic, and computational number theory, with a preference for connections to combinatorics but not an absolute requirement.

Why it fits:

- the argument is accessible and congruence-driven;
- no APC according to current DOAJ metadata;
- exact-depth counting and arithmetic-progressions aspects fit its scope.

Risk:

- less natural than Acta Arithmetica / Ramanujan Journal for the present theorem.

**Current ranking:** strong fallback if higher-tier specialist venues decline.

## Recommended sequence

Do not submit simultaneously.

After external review and priority audit:

1. **Acta Arithmetica** or **The Ramanujan Journal** (choose based on reviewer feedback about strength/novelty).
2. **Journal of Number Theory** if the paper is expanded and reviewer feedback is very strong.
3. **INTEGERS** as a credible fallback.

## Before journal submission

Required:

- incorporate the self-contained equivalence proof from `TYPEII_COORDINATE_PRIMARY_SOURCE_AUDIT.md`;
- obtain/consider at least one external mathematical review;
- check bibliography metadata and DOIs;
- post a stable arXiv version unless the author intentionally prefers journal-first;
- archive reproducibility scripts/certificates in a permanent public repository/DOI if possible;
- make the claim boundary explicit in abstract, introduction, and cover letter.
