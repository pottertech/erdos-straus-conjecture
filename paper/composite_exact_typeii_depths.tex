\title{An Infinite Composite-Modulus Exact-Depth Family for Type-II Erd\H{o}s--Straus Solutions}
\author{Skip Potter}
\date{Draft -- August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
For a prime $p\equiv1\pmod4$, let $\delta_{\rm sq}(p)$ be the least positive integer $A$ for which there is a divisor $D\mid A^2$ satisfying
\[
4A-1\mid p+4D.
\]
This square-completion coordinate is equivalent to the complete standard prime Type-II criterion for the Erd\H{o}s--Straus equation.

We prove an infinite exact-depth family with deliberately composite target modulus.  If $q\equiv5\pmod8$ is prime and
\[
A_q=\frac{3q+1}{4},
\]
then $4A_q-1=3q$ is composite and infinitely many primes satisfy
\[
\delta_{\rm sq}(p)=A_q.
\]
Indeed the target witness may always be chosen as $D=2$, and for every $q>5$ the prime family may be chosen inside the classical hard class $p\equiv1\pmod{840}$.  Consequently, if $N_{\rm comp}(X)$ counts composite-target depths $A\le X$ that are realized as $\delta_{\rm sq}(p)$ for infinitely many primes, then
\[
N_{\rm comp}(X)\ge\left(\frac13+o(1)\right)\frac{X}{\log X}.
\]

We also give a finite CRT certificate theorem for arbitrary composite target layers and use it to certify two more dependency-rich examples: at $A=649$ and $A=22339$, every realized divisor-modulus primitive branch supports infinitely many primes of exact square depth.  The symbolic family is unconditional and computation-free; the two full-layer examples are independently machine-verifiable finite certificates.

These results concern the exact-depth spectrum of Type-II solutions.  They do not prove universal Type-II solvability or the Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture asks whether
\[
\frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z
\]
has a solution in positive integers for every $n\ge2$.  It is enough to study prime denominators.  For a prime $p$, the Type-I/Type-II dichotomy is classical; complete formulations appear in modern work of Elsholtz--Tao and Pomerance--Weingartner, and Dahan recently reformulated the Type-II problem in a search-depth setting.

A historical Type-II coordinate, going back through the Th\'epault--Mizony--Gardes line, is obtained from a divisor of a square.  For positive integers $A,D$ one asks for
\[
D\mid A^2,\qquad 4A-1\mid p+4D. \tag{1}
\]
We use $A$ as a \emph{square-completion depth}.  The arithmetic mechanism in (1) is prior work; our subject is its exact first-hit spectrum.

Define
\[
m_A=4A-1,
\qquad
R_A=\{-4D\pmod{m_A}:D\mid A^2\},
\]
and, for primes $p\equiv1\pmod4$,
\[
\delta_{\rm sq}(p)
=\min\{A\ge1:p\bmod m_A\in R_A\}, \tag{2}
\]
with $\delta_{\rm sq}(p)=\infty$ if no Type-II witness exists.

A prime target modulus $m_A$ gives an elementary exact-depth backbone: one can force the target residue while placing the denominator in a safe residue at every earlier modulus.  Public 2026 work of the Free Computation Foundation/CENTL develops this prime-modulus backbone and a related minimal-depth spectrum.  Composite target moduli are harder because they share factors with earlier layers.

Our main observation is that a large infinite composite subfamily nevertheless has an equally elementary exact-depth construction.  If
\[
q\equiv5\pmod8\quad\text{is prime},
\qquad
A_q=\frac{3q+1}{4},
\]
then
\[
m_{A_q}=3q.
\]
The factor $3$ is the smallest possible $3\pmod4$ cofactor.  The new prime $q\equiv1\pmod4$ cannot occur in any earlier modulus $4B-1$, and the target residue from the fixed divisor $D=2$ agrees modulo $3$ with a universal safe residue.  This yields a simultaneous CRT construction avoiding every earlier Type-II layer.

The paper has two parts.  Sections 2--4 establish the symbolic infinite family and its quantitative consequences.  Sections 5--7 give a general finite CRT certificate for arbitrary composite layers and two computationally assisted full-layer examples at depths $649$ and $22339$.

\section{The square-completion coordinate is complete Type II}
We first record that (1) is not merely a sufficient subfamily of Type II.

A convenient complete prime Type-II criterion, in the notation used by Dahan, is the existence of positive integers $u,a$ and a divisor $s$ such that
\[
s\mid ap+u,
\qquad
s\equiv-1\pmod{4ua}. \tag{3}
\]

\begin{proposition}[equivalence of Type-II coordinates]
For a prime $p$, criterion \emph{(3)} holds if and only if there exist $A,D>0$ satisfying
\[
D\mid A^2,
\qquad
4A-1\mid p+4D. \tag{4}
\]
Thus the square-completion coordinate parametrizes the complete standard prime Type-II solution set.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that (3) holds.  Write
\[
s=4\alpha ua-1
\]
with $\alpha\ge1$, and set
\[
A=\alpha ua,
\qquad
D=\alpha u^2.
\]
Then $D\mid A^2$.  Because $s\mid ap+u$,
\[
4\alpha u(ap+u)=4Ap+4D\equiv p+4D\pmod{s},
\]
where $4A\equiv1\pmod{s}$.  Hence
\[
4A-1=s\mid p+4D.
\]

Conversely, suppose (4) holds.  Put
\[
E=\frac{A^2}{D},
\qquad
\alpha=\gcd(D,E).
\]
After dividing $D$ and $E$ by their gcd, the two resulting integers are coprime and their product is a square.  Hence there are coprime positive integers $u,a$ such that
\[
D=\alpha u^2,
\qquad
E=\alpha a^2,
\qquad
A=\alpha ua.
\]
Let
\[
s=4A-1=4\alpha ua-1.
\]
From $s\mid p+4D$, multiplying by $a$ gives
\[
ap+4aD=ap+4Au\equiv ap+u\pmod{s}.
\]
Thus $s\mid ap+u$, and by construction
\[
s\equiv-1\pmod{4ua}.
\]
This is (3).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The algebra above is a coordinate equivalence, not a novelty claim for Type II itself.  It is included so that statements about $\delta_{\rm sq}$ are transparently statements about the complete standard prime Type-II family.  This depth is nevertheless coordinate-specific and should not be conflated with Dahan's pair depth $\max(u,a)$.
\end{remark}

\section{A universal safe residue}
The construction rests on one elementary observation.

\begin{lemma}[universal safe residue]
For every $B\ge1$ and every divisor $E\mid B^2$,
\[
4B-1\nmid4E+1.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
1\notin R_B.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
g=\gcd(B,E),\qquad B=ga,\qquad E=gd,\qquad\gcd(a,d)=1.
\]
Since $E\mid B^2$, we have $d\mid g$; write $g=dh$.  Hence
\[
B=dha,
\qquad
E=d^2h.
\]
Suppose that $4B-1\mid4E+1$.  Since $4B\equiv1$ and $4E\equiv-1$ modulo $4B-1$,
\[
E\equiv-B\pmod{4B-1},
\]
so
\[
4B-1\mid E+B=dh(d+a).
\]
But $\gcd(4B-1,dh)=1$, because every prime divisor of $dh$ divides $B$.  Therefore
\[
4B-1\mid d+a.
\]
This is impossible since
\[
4B-1=4dha-1>d+a
\]
for positive $d,h,a$.
\end{proof}

\section{An infinite composite-modulus exact-depth backbone}
We now state the main theorem.

\begin{theorem}[composite exact-depth backbone]\label{thm:backbone}
Let $q\equiv5\pmod8$ be prime and put
\[
A_q=\frac{3q+1}{4}.
\]
Then
\[
4A_q-1=3q
\]
is composite, and there are infinitely many primes $p\equiv1\pmod4$ for which
\[
\boxed{\delta_{\rm sq}(p)=A_q.}
\]
The target witness may always be chosen as $D=2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $q\equiv5\pmod8$,
\[
3q+1\equiv16\pmod{24},
\]
and therefore
\[
A_q\equiv4\pmod6.
\]
In particular $A_q$ is even, so $2\mid A_q^2$.  Put
\[
m=4A_q-1=3q,
\qquad
a\equiv-8\pmod m.
\]
Every integer $n\equiv a\pmod m$ therefore has a depth-$A_q$ witness with $D=2$.

Consider an earlier depth $B<A_q$, with $m_B=4B-1$.  We claim that $q\nmid m_B$.  If $m_B=qs$, then $m_B\equiv3\pmod4$ and $q\equiv1\pmod4$ force $s\equiv3\pmod4$, so $s\ge3$.  Hence
\[
m_B\ge3q=m,
\]
contradicting $B<A_q$.  Consequently
\[
\gcd(m,m_B)\mid3
\]
for every $B<A_q$.

Let
\[
L=\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le B<A_q}(4B-1).
\]
Since $a=-8\equiv1\pmod3$, the congruence system
\[
n\equiv-8\pmod{3q},
\qquad
n\equiv1\pmod L,
\qquad
n\equiv1\pmod4 \tag{5}
\]
is CRT-compatible.  Let $n\equiv n_0\pmod M$ be its solution, where
\[
M=\operatorname{lcm}(4,3q,L).
\]

For every $B<A_q$, system (5) gives
\[
n\equiv1\pmod{4B-1}.
\]
By the universal safe-residue lemma, no member of the progression has a square-completed Type-II witness at an earlier depth.  At depth $A_q$, however,
\[
n\equiv-8\pmod{3q}
\]
and $D=2\mid A_q^2$, so the target witness exists.

The progression is reduced.  Every prime factor inherited from $L$ sees residue $1$; the new odd prime factor $q$ sees residue $-8\not\equiv0\pmod q$; and the residue modulo $4$ is $1$.  Hence
\[
\gcd(n_0,M)=1.
\]
Dirichlet's theorem supplies infinitely many primes in this progression, all congruent to $1\pmod4$, and every one has exact square depth $A_q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[infinitely many composite exact depths]
The set of composite-target square depths realized by infinitely many primes is infinite and unbounded.  More precisely,
\[
\left\{\frac{3q+1}{4}:q\text{ prime},\ q\equiv5\pmod8\right\}
\subseteq\mathcal D^{\rm comp}_{\infty}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[quantitative lower bound]\label{cor:count}
Let
\[
N_{\rm comp}(X)
=\#\{A\le X:4A-1\text{ is composite and }\delta_{\rm sq}(p)=A
\text{ for infinitely many primes }p\}.
\]
Then
\[
N_{\rm comp}(X)
\ge
\pi\!\left(\frac{4X-1}{3};8,5\right)
=\left(\frac13+o(1)\right)\frac{X}{\log X}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:backbone} gives one distinct depth for every prime $q\equiv5\pmod8$ with $q\le(4X-1)/3$.  The prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions gives
\[
\pi(Y;8,5)\sim\frac14\operatorname{Li}(Y),
\]
and $Y\sim4X/3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[classical hard-core realization]
For every prime $q\equiv5\pmod8$ with $q>5$, infinitely many primes
\[
p\equiv1\pmod{840}
\]
satisfy
\[
\delta_{\rm sq}(p)=\frac{3q+1}{4}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $q>5$ in this residue class,
\[
\gcd(3q,840)=3.
\]
The target residue $-8$ and the hard residue $1$ agree modulo $3$.  Replacing the congruence $n\equiv1\pmod4$ in (5) by $n\equiv1\pmod{840}$ therefore preserves CRT compatibility and reducedness.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[small regression example]
For $q=13$, Theorem~\ref{thm:backbone} gives $A_q=10$, target modulus $39$, and witness $D=2$.  One prime in the hard-core progression is
\[
10{,}139{,}116{,}681,
\]
and direct enumeration through depth $10$ confirms that its first square-completed Type-II witness is $(A,D)=(10,2)$.  This numerical check is not used in the proof.
\end{remark}

\section{A finite certificate for arbitrary composite layers}
The $3q$ backbone exploits a special gcd pattern.  For a general composite target modulus one can still reduce exact first-hit to a finite congruence certificate.

Fix a target pair $(A,D)$ with $D\mid A^2$, and put
\[
m_A=4A-1,
\qquad
a\equiv-4D\pmod{m_A}.
\]
Write the target progression as
\[
n=a+m_Ak. \tag{6}
\]
An earlier depth $B<A$, with $E\mid B^2$, has trap progression
\[
n\equiv-4E\pmod{m_B},
\qquad m_B=4B-1. \tag{7}
\]
Let
\[
g_{A,B}=\gcd(m_A,m_B),
\qquad
q_{A,B}=\frac{m_B}{g_{A,B}}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[induced parameter congruence]
The target progression (6) intersects (7) if and only if
\[
a\equiv-4E\pmod{g_{A,B}}.
\]
When compatible, the intersection removes exactly one residue class
\[
k\equiv c_{B,E}\pmod{q_{A,B}},
\]
where
\[
c_{B,E}\equiv
\frac{-4E-a}{g_{A,B}}
\left(\frac{m_A}{g_{A,B}}\right)^{-1}
\pmod{q_{A,B}}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitute (6) into (7), divide the resulting linear congruence by $g_{A,B}$, and invert the remaining coefficient modulo $q_{A,B}$.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal C_{A,D}$ be the finite set of all forbidden pairs $(q,c)$ obtained in this way for $B<A$ and $E\mid B^2$.  Put
\[
L=\operatorname{lcm}\{q:(q,c)\in\mathcal C_{A,D}\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[finite exact-depth certificate]
Suppose a residue class $k\equiv r\pmod L$ avoids every forbidden class in $\mathcal C_{A,D}$ and the resulting denominator progression
\[
n\equiv a+m_Ar\pmod{m_AL}
\]
is reduced.  Then infinitely many primes have exact square depth $A$ with the target witness $D$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every member of the progression has the target depth-$A$ witness and avoids every earlier Type-II trap.  Reducedness and Dirichlet's theorem give infinitely many primes in the progression.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[prime-power certificates]
In practice $L$ may be enormous.  It is enough to specify $r$ by its maximal prime-power coordinates.  A forbidden congruence is then a forbidden tuple across the prime-power factors of its modulus.  The accompanying verifiers reconstruct every earlier trap from first principles and check a compact coordinate assignment rather than storing a gigantic CRT integer.
\end{remark}

\section{Full composite layers beyond the elementary backbone}
The infinite family in Theorem~\ref{thm:backbone} has target modulus $3q$.  More dependency-rich composite layers also occur.

\subsection{Depth 649}
Take
\[
A=649=11\cdot59,
\qquad
m_A=2595=3\cdot5\cdot173.
\]
Among the nine divisors of $649^2$, divisor-modulus ancestry leaves exactly four realized primitive branches,
\[
D\in\{11,59,7139,38291\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[full composite layer at 649]
Every one of these four branches supports infinitely many primes $p$ satisfying
\[
\delta_{\rm sq}(p)=649.
\]
The four families may be placed respectively in the classical hard classes
\[
1,169,169,121\pmod{840}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The deterministic verifier \texttt{exact\_depth\_649\_full\_layer\_certificate.py} reconstructs every earlier trap $B<649$, converts compatible intersections to forbidden $k$-classes, and checks four compact prime-power assignments satisfying the finite certificate theorem.  No precomputed trap list is loaded.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Depth 22339}
Take
\[
A=22339=89\cdot251,
\qquad
m_A=89355=3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot23\cdot37.
\]
The four realized primitive branches are
\[
D\in\{89,251,22361,67079\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[full composite layer at 22339]
Every one of these four branches supports infinitely many primes $p$ satisfying
\[
\delta_{\rm sq}(p)=22339.
\]
The families may be placed respectively in the classical hard classes
\[
169,361,121,169\pmod{840}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The deterministic verifier \texttt{exact\_depth\_22339\_full\_layer\_certificate.py} reconstructs all earlier layers $B<22339$ and checks the stored compact prime-power certificates.  Depending on the branch, the construction contains $14210$ or $14256$ distinct reduced parameter moduli and $576465$ or $577670$ forbidden parameter classes on $5703$ maximal prime-power coordinates.
\end{proof}

\section{Reproducibility and audit boundary}
The main theorem and Corollary~\ref{cor:count} are symbolic and require no computation beyond standard primality input for the parameter $q$.

The two full-layer examples are computationally assisted finite theorems.  Their verifier scripts regenerate the complete earlier trap system from the definition $E\mid B^2$ and check compact certificates independently.  During manuscript preparation, an error was found in an optional mod-$840$ compatibility assertion in the depth-$649$ verifier: the overlap between a prime-power coordinate and the reduced hard-core modulus must be tested modulo their gcd, not their minimum.  The assertion was corrected; all four certificates pass, and the exact-depth assignments themselves were unchanged.

For publication, the repository should be archived at a permanent DOI and the certificate scripts should be accompanied by checksums and a minimal independent verifier.

\section{Relation to prior work and novelty boundary}
The Type-II mechanism is prior mathematics.  Mizony--Gardes explicitly record the square-divisor condition and a corresponding unit-fraction identity.  Elsholtz--Tao develop the Type-I/Type-II framework, Pomerance--Weingartner give complete criteria, and Dahan studies Type-II search depth analytically.

Public August 2026 work of FCF/CENTL develops a minimal-depth spectrum with an infinite prime-modulus exact-depth backbone, structural-gap families, and a square-completion/shadow program.  That work explicitly leaves a dependency-rich composite residual spectrum to classify.

The candidate contribution here is narrower and should be described conservatively:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the explicit infinite composite-modulus exact-depth family
\[
A=(3q+1)/4,\qquad q\equiv5\pmod8\text{ prime},
\]
with the quantitative lower bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:count};
\item realization of that family inside the classical hard class $1\pmod{840}$;
\item the finite exact-depth certificate formulation for arbitrary composite target layers; and
\item the two full composite-layer certificates at $649$ and $22339$ as examples outside the elementary $3q$ construction.
\end{enumerate}

A targeted literature search through August 2026 did not locate the exact $3q$ family or the full-layer statements above.  This negative search does not establish priority.  A broader historical review, including non-arXiv and non-English sources, is required before claiming first discovery.

\section{Open questions}
The symbolic family immediately raises several questions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Can the factor $3$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:backbone} be replaced by other fixed $3\pmod4$ cofactors while preserving a universal earlier-layer avoidance residue?
\item Are there additional infinite families obtained from fixed divisors $D>2$?
\item What is the true order of $N_{\rm comp}(X)$?  Corollary~\ref{cor:count} gives only a lower bound of order $X/\log X$.
\item Are there infinitely many \emph{full} composite layers, in the sense verified here at $649$ and $22339$?
\item How does $\delta_{\rm sq}$ compare quantitatively with Dahan's pair depth on the same prime families?
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Claim boundary}
Nothing here proves that every prime has a Type-II solution.  Exact-depth realization is compatible with the existence of primes with no Type-II solution.  The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture remains open.
