\begin{abstract}
For primes $p\equiv1\pmod4$, let
\[
\sqdepth(p)=\min\{A\ge1:\exists D\mid A^2,\ 4A-1\mid p+4D\},
\]
the first-hit depth in the classical divisor-square coordinate for prime Type-II Erd\H{o}s--Straus solutions. The parametrization itself is classical; we study the exact-depth spectrum.

We first recall a composite $3q$ backbone and a half-dimensional sieve showing that almost every associated prime parameter yields an exact depth. We then classify the first occurrence of the Jacobi characters attached to $4A-1$: new character directions arise only at prime target moduli and at $3q$ target moduli. Thus $3q$ is maximal for constructions that avoid earlier layers solely by imposing Jacobi sign $+1$.

The main new mechanism goes beyond that barrier. If a target residue is quadratic-residue at every fixed prime coordinate and nonresidue at one variable prime $r$, all earlier layers not containing $r$ are eliminated simultaneously. For a fixed cofactor $s$, the remaining $r$-bearing layers reduce to the finite Diophantine equation
\[
r(kh-dc^2)+k+dh-2dc=0.
\]
This yields an admissible fixed-cofactor theorem: under explicit local character and equality-free congruence conditions, all but finitely many prime parameters in a fixed progression generate exact depths $A=(sr+1)/4$ realized by infinitely many primes.

In particular, for every fixed prime $s>3$ with $s\equiv3\pmod8$, all but finitely many primes
\[
r\equiv s+2\pmod{2(s+1)}
\]
give exact depths $A=(sr+1)/4$. Hence there are infinitely many post-Jacobi fixed cofactors. Combining this hierarchy with the prime/$3q$ backbones and several explicit $7\pmod8$ families gives the rigorous numerical bound
\[
\liminf_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_\infty(X)\log X}{X}\ge2.887684329\ldots,
\]
where $N_\infty(X)$ counts depth values $A\le X$ realized as exact square-completed Type-II depth by infinitely many primes.

These results concern the exact Type-II depth spectrum; they do not prove universal Type-II solvability or the Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture asks whether
\[
\frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z
\]
has a solution in positive integers for every $n\ge2$. For prime denominators, standard solutions split into Type I and Type II. We study Type II through the classical divisor-square coordinate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square}
D\mid A^2,\qquad 4A-1\mid p+4D.
\end{equation}
The mechanism belongs to the Th\'epault--Mizony lineage and is not claimed here. Modern complete Type-II formulations appear in work of Elsholtz--Tao and Pomerance--Weingartner; Dahan studies Type-II sieve dimension and search depth.

For primes $p\equiv1\pmod4$, define
\[
\sqdepth(p)=\min\{A\ge1:\exists D\mid A^2\text{ satisfying \eqref{eq:square}}\}.
\]
Let
\[
N_\infty(X)=\#\{A\le X:\text{ infinitely many primes }p\text{ satisfy }\sqdepth(p)=A\}.
\]
Our goal is structural: which depth layers can be realized infinitely often, and what arithmetic mechanisms force exact first occurrence?

\section{The square coordinate and a universal Jacobi obstruction}
\begin{proposition}[complete prime Type-II coordinate]\label{prop:complete}
For a prime $p\equiv1\pmod4$, a standard Type-II Erd\H{o}s--Straus solution exists if and only if there are positive integers $A,D$ such that
\[
D\mid A^2,\qquad p\nmid D,\qquad 4A-1\mid p+4D.
\]
\end{proposition}

For $B\ge1$, put
\[
m_B=4B-1,
\qquad
R_B=\{-4E\pmod{m_B}:E\mid B^2\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Jacobi obstruction]\label{lem:jacobi}
For every $E\mid B^2$,
\[
\boxed{\left(\frac{-4E}{m_B}\right)=-1.}
\]
Hence no residue of Jacobi sign $+1$ modulo $m_B$ belongs to $R_B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every prime divisor of $B$ is coprime to $m_B$. Quadratic reciprocity applied to $4B\equiv1\pmod{m_B}$ gives $(E/m_B)=1$ for every $E\mid B^2$ (with the supplementary law handling the factor $2$). Since $m_B\equiv3\pmod4$, one has $(-1/m_B)=-1$, while $4$ is a square.
\end{proof}

\section{The composite $3q$ backbone}
\begin{theorem}[composite $3q$ exact-depth theorem]\label{thm:3q}
Let $q\equiv1\pmod4$ be prime and put
\[
A_q=\frac{3q+1}{4}.
\]
If there exists $D\mid A_q^2$ with $D\equiv2\pmod3$, then infinitely many primes $p$ satisfy
\[
\boxed{\sqdepth(p)=A_q.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The target modulus is $3q$ and the target residue is $-4D\equiv1\pmod3$. If $B<A_q$, then $q\nmid4B-1$: otherwise $4B-1=qt$ with $t\equiv3\pmod4$, hence $t\ge3$ and $4B-1\ge3q=4A_q-1$. Thus every earlier modulus meets $3q$ only in a divisor of $3$. CRT can therefore impose the target condition modulo $3q$ and residue $1$ modulo every earlier modulus. Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi} excludes all earlier layers, and Dirichlet gives infinitely many primes in a reduced refinement.
\end{proof}

A half-dimensional upper-bound sieve applied to $A_q=(3q+1)/4$ gives
\[
E(Y)\ll Y(\log Y)^{-3/2}
\]
for the prime parameters $q\equiv1\pmod4$ for which $A_q$ has no prime divisor $2\pmod3$. In particular the $3q$ family contributes
\[
\left(\frac23+o(1)\right)\frac{X}{\log X}
\]
composite exact depths.

\section{Jacobi-span maximality}
Write
\[
v_A=(\operatorname{ord}_r(4A-1)\bmod2)_r
\]
for the squarefree parity vector of $4A-1$.

\begin{theorem}[first-occurrence span classification]\label{thm:span}
Let
\[
V_{A-1}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbf F_2}\{v_B:B<A\}.
\]
Then $v_A\notin V_{A-1}$ if and only if either
\[
4A-1=q\equiv3\pmod4\quad\text{is prime},
\]
or
\[
4A-1=3q,
\qquad q\equiv1\pmod4\quad\text{is prime}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If a prime $r\equiv3\pmod4$ occurs to odd exponent in $4A-1$, then the coordinate $e_r$ already occurs at depth $(r+1)/4$, unless the target itself is $r$. If $r\equiv1\pmod4$, the earlier modulus $3r$ gives $e_3+e_r$ and depth $1$ gives $e_3$, unless the target itself is $3r$. Thus every target coordinate is generated earlier outside the stated cases; conversely the new prime coordinate cannot appear earlier in either exceptional case.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Jacobi-safe maximality]\label{cor:jacmax}
A global CRT construction that avoids every earlier layer solely by imposing Jacobi sign $+1$ can have a composite target only when
\[
4A-1=3q,\qquad q\equiv1\pmod4\text{ prime}.
\]
\end{corollary}

Thus the next exact-depth mechanisms must use information finer than global Jacobi sign.

\section{A single nonsquare coordinate}
\begin{lemma}[single-nonsquare reduction]\label{lem:single}
Let the target modulus be $m_A$ and let the target residue $a=-4D$ be a quadratic residue at every prime coordinate of $m_A$ except one prime $r$, where it is a nonresidue. Then all earlier layers whose modulus is not divisible by $r$ can be avoided simultaneously by a CRT choice that is square at every prime-power coordinate not involving $r$. Consequently exact-depth realization reduces to the earlier layers whose moduli contain $r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At every prime-power coordinate away from $r$, choose a square residue compatible with the target on the common factors of $m_A$. Any earlier modulus not divisible by $r$ then sees a unit square, hence Jacobi sign $+1$, and is excluded by Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi}.
\end{proof}

This lemma collapses the formerly large branch-dependent certificates at $A=649$ and $A=22339$ to finite checks on only the earlier layers containing the unique nonsquare target coordinate.

\section{The fixed-cofactor collision equation}
Fix an odd cofactor $s\equiv3\pmod4$, a positive integer $d$, and a variable prime $r$. Put
\[
A=\frac{sr+1}{4}
\]
and suppose $d\mid A$. The target residue is $-4d$ modulo $sr$. Assume it is square on the fixed coordinates dividing $s$ and nonsquare at $r$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:single}, only earlier moduli divisible by $r$ remain.

An earlier $r$-bearing modulus has the form
\[
4B-1=cr,
\qquad 0<c<s,
\qquad c\equiv3\pmod4.
\]

\begin{lemma}[exceptional-layer equation]\label{lem:master}
If the depth-$B$ trap intersects the target branch, then there are nonnegative integers $k,h$ satisfying
\[
\boxed{r(kh-dc^2)+k+dh-2dc=0}
\]
and
\[
16d\mid rh+1.
\]
If the AM--GM equality case is excluded, then necessarily
\[
\boxed{kh<dc^2.}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A collision gives $E\mid B^2$ with $E\equiv d\pmod r$, so write $E=rk+d$. If $F=B^2/E$, the complementary congruence can be written $16dF=rh+1$. Multiplying yields
\[
(rk+d)(rh+1)=d(cr+1)^2,
\]
which gives the displayed equation. If $kh>dc^2$, AM--GM yields $k+dh>2dc$, contradicting the sign of the equation. Equality forces $k=dc$ and $h=c$, equivalently $16d\mid cr+1$.
\end{proof}

The crucial feature is that, once $s,d,c$ are fixed and equality is excluded, the region $kh<dc^2$ is finite. Each pair $(k,h)$ determines at most one rational parameter
\[
r=\frac{k+dh-2dc}{dc^2-kh}.
\]
Hence every fixed exceptional cofactor creates only finitely many collision primes.

\section{Admissible fixed-cofactor backbones}
\begin{theorem}[fixed-cofactor finite-exception theorem]\label{thm:fixed}
Fix $s\equiv3\pmod4$ and $d$ with $\gcd(s,4d)=1$. Suppose a reduced prime progression for $r$ is chosen such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $r\equiv-s^{-1}\pmod{4d}$, so $d\mid(sr+1)/4$;
\item $(-d/q)=+1$ for every prime $q\mid s$;
\item $(-d/r)=-1$ throughout the chosen prime progression;
\item the equality congruence $16d\mid cr+1$ is excluded for every $0<c<s$, $c\equiv3\pmod4$.
\end{enumerate}
Then all but finitely many prime parameters in that progression generate a depth
\[
\boxed{A=(sr+1)/4}
\]
that is realized as exact square-completed Type-II depth by infinitely many primes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditions (1)--(3) give a single nonsquare target coordinate, so Lemma~\ref{lem:single} removes every earlier modulus not containing $r$. There are only finitely many remaining cofactors $c<s$. Condition (4) excludes the equality case in Lemma~\ref{lem:master}; hence every $c$ contributes only finitely many collision parameters. Remove their finite union and apply Dirichlet first to the parameter progression and then to the resulting reduced exact-depth CRT progression.
\end{proof}

\section{An infinite post-Jacobi hierarchy}
\begin{theorem}[canonical fixed-cofactor hierarchy]\label{thm:hier}
For every fixed prime
\[
s>3,\qquad s\equiv3\pmod8,
\]
put
\[
d=\frac{s+1}{2}.
\]
Then all but finitely many primes
\[
\boxed{r\equiv s+2\pmod{2(s+1)}}
\]
generate exact depths
\[
\boxed{A=\frac{sr+1}{4}}
\]
that are each realized by infinitely many primes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The parameter congruence gives $d\mid A$. Modulo $s$, the target residue is $-2$, and $(-2/s)=1$ for $s\equiv3\pmod8$. Writing $d=2m$ with $m=(s+1)/4$ odd, the same parameter congruence gives $r\equiv5\pmod8$ and $r\equiv1$ modulo every odd prime divisor of $m$. Hence $(-d/r)=-1$ by the supplementary laws and quadratic reciprocity.

For an exceptional cofactor $c<s$, the equality case would require
\[
8(s+1)\mid cr+1.
\]
But modulo $s+1$ one has $r\equiv1$, so this would force $c+1\equiv0\pmod{s+1}$, impossible for $0<c<s$. Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed} now applies.
\end{proof}

This theorem is strictly beyond Corollary~\ref{cor:jacmax}: except at the already known $3q$ layer, these target characters are Jacobi-dependent on earlier layers, yet finer local square information still yields exact first-hit prime families.

\section{Counting the hierarchy}
For fixed $s$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:hier}, the parameter family contributes
\[
\frac{4}{s\varphi(2(s+1))}\frac{X}{\log X}
\]
depths up to $X$. Define
\[
C_{3(8)}=
\sum_{\substack{s>3\ \mathrm{prime}\\s\equiv3\ (8)}}
\frac{4}{s\varphi(2(s+1))}.
\]
The series converges because its summand is $\ll(\log\log s)/s^2$.

\begin{theorem}[spectrum lower bound]\label{thm:count}
Let
\[
C_{\rm extra}=\frac17+\frac1{184}+\frac1{248}+\frac1{987},
\]
coming from the independently proved fixed-cofactor families with $s=7,23,31,47$. Then
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{X\to\infty}
\frac{N_\infty(X)\log X}{X}
\ge
\frac83+C_{3(8)}+C_{\rm extra}.
}
\]
In particular, using only hierarchy cofactors $s\le100000$,
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{X\to\infty}
\frac{N_\infty(X)\log X}{X}
\ge2.887684329\ldots .
}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any fixed finite set of cofactors, the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions applies to each family separately. Distinct fixed cofactors give distinct semiprime target moduli except for finitely many swapped-prime coincidences, which do not affect the asymptotic. Thus the contributions add for every fixed finite set. Taking the supremum over finite subsets gives the convergent-series term $C_{3(8)}$ without invoking any uniformity in a cofactor growing with $X$. The displayed numerical value uses the exact partial sum
\[
\sum_{\substack{s\le100000\ \mathrm{prime}\\s\equiv3\ (8)}}
\frac{4}{s\varphi(2(s+1))}
=0.0676803077377\ldots .
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Statistical multiplicity on the $3q$ backbone}
For $A_q=(3q+1)/4$, define
\[
\nu_-(A_q)=\#\{\ell\mid A_q:\ell\text{ prime},\ \ell\equiv2\pmod3\}.
\]
Every such witness prime yields a distinct infinite exact-depth progression. Applying a standard sieve-theoretic Erd\H{o}s--Kac theorem for additive functions on prime-generated sequences gives
\[
\frac{\nu_-(A_q)-\frac12\log\log q}
{\sqrt{\frac12\log\log q}}
\Longrightarrow N(0,1)
\]
over primes $q\equiv1\pmod4$. This probability theorem is not claimed as new; the contribution is its exact-depth interpretation.

\section{Computational examples beyond the symbolic hierarchy}
The accompanying repository also contains exact reduced certificates for the full composite layers $A=649$ and $A=22339$. The single-nonsquare-coordinate reduction substantially simplifies both: at $649$ only three earlier layers require explicit local checks; at $22339$ only the earlier layers containing the unique bad prime coordinate remain. These examples are supporting evidence for richer post-Jacobi structure, not inputs to Theorems~\ref{thm:hier} or~\ref{thm:count}.

\section{Prior work and claim boundaries}
The divisor-square Type-II parametrization, quadratic reciprocity, Dirichlet's theorem, prime number theorems in arithmetic progressions, Bombieri--Vinogradov, half-dimensional sieve machinery, and Erd\H{o}s--Kac theory are prior mathematics. Contemporary FCF/CENTL work contains closely related divisor-Jacobi, reciprocity-signature, ancestry, shadow, and exact-depth machinery. Their public research program also explicitly separates exact-depth realization from the unresolved all-prime Erd\H{o}s--Straus problem.

The candidate contributions here are the exact-depth fixed-cofactor finite-exception mechanism, the canonical infinite post-Jacobi hierarchy, and the resulting spectrum-count synthesis, together with the earlier $3q$/Jacobi-span results used to expose the barrier that the hierarchy crosses. Targeted searches through August 2026 have not located these exact statements; this negative search does not establish priority.
